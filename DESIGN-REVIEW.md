# The Power Codex: design review

The reference has a coherent classical identity: dark stone, ivory typography, restrained gold, and a clear learn–apply–reflect journey. These are the strongest ideas to preserve.

## Changes applied

- Larger reading text, stronger contrast, and touch-sized controls replace the miniature type in the reference.
- The top navigation names the primary flows; journal, profile, and settings remain available across pages and in the mobile menu.
- Search, category filters, empty states, and previous/next navigation make all 48 laws usable as an archive.
- The analyzer explains that it matches themes through rules and distinguishes specific matches from general guidance.
- Ten original simulation scenarios use Laws 1, 9, 13, 23, 29, 35, 39, 43, 45, and 47 from the supplied PDF. Each choice has specific feedback, a practice score, and a related-law link.
- The profile starts with six reflection questions and displays counts from the answers; it does not invent personality percentages.
- Notes, reflections, profile answers, and session results save to a database under the authenticated user. Empty and failed-save states are explicit.
- The unverified quotation credited to Robert Greene in the reference is replaced with a verified law title.

## Content approach

Law titles follow Robert Greene’s The 48 Laws of Power. Explanations, examples, and simulation questions are original commentary grounded in the user-supplied PDF. Modern applications and caveats are labeled so they are distinguishable from Greene’s argument. The full PDF is not distributed with the website.

## Language

The user requested Taglish for accessibility. Law explanations, scenarios, choices, outcome feedback, quiz content, and key guidance now use natural Taglish. Original English law titles remain for cross-reference. The analyzer recognizes selected Filipino and English terms.
